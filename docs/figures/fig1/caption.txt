Fig. 1. Adding a skill to one policy network without changing an earlier one. (a) The Pick expert (three 256-unit tanh layers on the 42-d state x; head W_old) is frozen. A small adapter g(x, h2) (298→64→256) writes into its last hidden layer through a fixed projector: h′ = h + Pg, P = I − BᵀB, where B is an orthonormal basis of the rows of all earlier heads (one SVD). Every head reads h′, so one forward pass serves all skills. Because W_old P = 0, Pick's actions are unchanged at every state without Pick data or replay. Over 8 runs the measured change is ≤ 1.5 × 10⁻⁶ on 102,400 stored Pick states (≤ 2.1 × 10⁻⁶ on 8,192 random states); without P, the same trained adapters would change them by 0.52–0.83. Only g and the new head W_new (initialised as a copy of W_old) are trained, with PPO. Each further skill adds one projected adapter, leaving 256 − 8k free directions after k skills. (b) PlaceCube success (256 episodes every 102,400 steps), median and IQR over 8 seeds; runs stop after three consecutive evaluations ≥ 0.9 and carry their last value forward. Prefix-sum is the same adapter with P = I; either its last layer starts at zero (zero-init) or it starts from our exact initial network (matched init), which then differs from ours only in the projection during training. Bottom: steps to 90% (first of three consecutive evaluations ≥ 0.9), median and range. Ours is ≥ 2 evaluations faster in all 16 seed pairs (pre-registered exact one-sided signed-rank test, p = 0.0039 per variant). (c) Pick success on 256 fixed-seed episodes at the final checkpoint, Pick head reading the shared h′; n = runs evaluated so far. Prefix-sum keeps Pick exact only if Pick reads its own adapter level, which gives up the shared vector. †Earlier code version, 3 seeds; the original Pick head reads the fine-tuned trunk.
